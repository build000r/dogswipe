# DogSwipe Dueling Wizard Scores - Codex

Date: 2026-06-17

Scoring rubric: 0 means actively harmful or invariant-breaking; 1000 means unusually strong, practical, hard to game, legally/ethically aligned, and worth making foundational. I scored the named ideas in both lists, including rejected candidate ideas where the model named them, because the prompt asked for each idea.

## Claude Ideas (CC)

| Model / Idea | Score | Candid evaluation | Biggest risk or flaw |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| CC - Founding-member signup grant | 520 | Useful only if pre-funded at par and tied to real local participation. As a pure signup grant it is too easy to farm and does not prove supply or demand. | Sybil farming and idle balances before any real hand-off. |
| CC - Reciprocal onboarding pairs | 430 | The social idea is sound, but it creates a manual matching problem before the product has density. It is more onboarding choreography than durable tokenomics. | High coordination cost for a small liquidity gain. |
| CC - Seller-issued scrip / store coupons | 500 | Coupons are useful if they remain discounts, not balances; seller-issued transferable scrip would fragment the unit of account and look cash-like. The idea is salvageable only as nontransferable maker coupons. | Accidentally creating many mini-currencies or pseudo-cash instruments. |
| CC - Invite-and-grant chain | 360 | Viral grants sound attractive, but grants tied to invites are one of the most farmable cold-start tools. It is worse than hand-off-gated referral rewards. | Multi-account referral loops. |
| CC - Neighborhood launch event | 650 | High-touch launch events are genuinely useful for liquidity because they synchronize supply and demand in time. It is not scalable software tokenomics, but it may be exactly right for the first few cells. | Operational load and no-show risk. |
| CC - Capped production bonus with time decay | 470 | A cap is necessary, but time decay on the bonus can punish reliable makers for being consistently useful. Balance-aware or budget-aware bonus curves are cleaner. | Discouraging the best makers during sustained activity. |
| CC - Community contribution minting | 320 | Broad "community contribution" minting is too subjective unless tightly scoped to verified app events. It invites admin favoritism and hard-to-audit issuance. | Ungameable verification is missing. |
| CC - Seasonal/event minting | 450 | Time-boxed events can create liveliness, but calendar-based minting is weak unless funded by an issuance budget. It is flavor, not a monetary foundation. | Minting because of a date rather than real supply/demand. |
| CC - Matched purchase bonus ("buy 10 get 12") | 560 | This can be useful if the extra 2 credits are pre-funded community lots, but as written it is mostly a discount. It helps first purchase conversion more than community reciprocity. | Inflationary discounting without proving real hand-offs. |
| CC - Referral minting on first hand-off | 660 | Hand-off-gated referrals are one of the better growth incentives because they require real activity, not signup spam. Still, they need strict caps and counterpart diversity checks. | Referral farms that manufacture low-quality hand-offs. |
| CC - Flat demurrage (uniform holding tax) | 430 | Economically coherent but too blunt for credits bought 1:1 with dollars. It creates major user-trust and gift-card/prepaid-law concerns if applied to purchased credits. | Users experience it as taking bought value. |
| CC - Use-it-or-lose-it hard expiry | 160 | This is the worst velocity lever for purchased credits: it feels confiscatory and is legally dangerous in gift-card-like regimes. It should only ever be considered for clearly promotional, non-purchased lots after counsel review. | Violating user trust and possibly consumer-protection rules. |
| CC - Tiered participation benefits | 520 | Badges and priority can reinforce culture with low economic risk. They are not strong enough to solve hoarding by themselves. | Becomes cosmetic gamification instead of liquidity. |
| CC - Community-wide velocity bonus | 280 | Aggregate velocity rewards suffer from free-rider problems and are hard for individuals to understand or influence. It adds accounting complexity for weak behavioral effect. | Users cannot connect their action to the reward. |
| CC - Contribution score / karma parallel system | 350 | A reputation layer is useful, but a second quasi-balance competes with credits and confuses incentives. Better to fold reciprocity signals into ranking and trust. | Two currencies by another name. |
| CC - Spending streaks with multiplier | 500 | Spend streaks can nudge velocity, especially if funded from a capped community pool. They are easy to game unless they require meaningful, diverse, completed claims. | Circular micro-claims for streak farming. |
| CC - Scaling production bonus for net producers | 240 | This points in the wrong direction if "net producer" already means surplus accumulation. It rewards the balance dead-end rather than relieving it. | Accelerates unspendable producer balances. |
| CC - Credit gifting to newcomers (direct) | 480 | Neighbor gifting fits the ethos, but direct transferable credits start to resemble peer transfer and invite off-platform sales. One-hop sponsored claims are safer. | Gifting becomes a cash-like transfer rail. |
| CC - Community project spending | 500 | Strong community fit, but it needs real-world governance, procurement, and fulfillment before it can be trusted. It is a later-stage sink, not a launch mechanism. | Logistics and governance swamp the credit mechanic. |
| CC - Maker-to-maker exchange surfacing | 650 | This is understated but important: producers need better ways to find things they actually want. It is more product discovery than tokenomics, but it directly reduces surplus frustration. | If supply remains thin, surfacing cannot create desired goods. |
| CC - Producer recognition / hall of fame | 420 | Social recognition supports community culture and costs little. It does not materially solve the economic dead-end without real spending paths. | Vanity rewards substitute for liquidity. |
| CC - Prepaid-card regulatory structure | 580 | Legal structure is not a user-facing mechanism, but compliance architecture is load-bearing for this product. The score is capped because "prepaid card" framing may be the wrong analogy and can import cash-out/fee constraints. | Choosing a legal analogy that worsens obligations. |
| CC - Per-city float isolation | 690 | Operationally strong: it limits contagion, improves reconciliation, and supports local governance. It is not sufficient alone, but it is a serious compliance and accounting primitive. | Fragmented liquidity and more accounting overhead. |
| CC - Credit-to-effort anchor ("1 credit = 15 min") | 340 | Philosophically appealing for mutual aid, but effort varies wildly by category, ingredients, skill, and culture. It would be unenforceable and would fight the par-dollar unit. | A false anchor that users reject in real pricing. |
| CC - Anti-inflation price ceiling per category | 260 | Hard ceilings are blunt, political, and likely to suppress high-quality or scarce offerings. Price guidance beats central control. | Admin price-setting fights and supply exit. |
| CC - Neighborhood Kindling | 760 | The staged subsidy sequence is strong and correctly pre-funds most credits at par. The "First Bite Free" stage needs revision because the invariant says users must hold credits to participate; model it as a grant/coupon issued before claim, not a zero-credit claim. | Free claims can violate the hold-credits invariant and attract farming. |
| CC - Spend-to-Earn Curve | 845 | This is one of the smartest incentive mechanisms in the set: it uses the already-decided production mint to reward circulation without demurraging bought credits. It is practical to implement, but needs pair/fraud controls and careful UX so high-balance makers do not feel punished. | Balance dumping through circular or alt-account claims. |
| CC - Pay It Forward | 820 | Strong ethos, strong surplus relief, and practical ledger implementation. It should be implemented as sponsored claims or one-hop vouchers, not general credit transfer. | Sponsored claims can crowd out paid claims or become reciprocal bonus washing. |
| CC - Dormancy-Triggered Demurrage with Community Renewal | 540 | The abandoned-credit problem is real, and the community-renewal framing is good. But broad demurrage on balances bought at par is legally and emotionally risky; this should be limited to promotional/minted lots unless counsel explicitly clears more. | Applying inactivity charges to purchased credits. |
| CC - Float-as-Community-Trust with Transparent Stewardship | 810 | The trust, segregation, transparency, and shutdown plan are essential. I score it below Grok's version because allocating 60% of float interest to platform operations blurs the "float is not revenue" ethos and may complicate the regulatory story. | Interest-to-operations can look like monetizing the float. |

## Grok Ideas (GROK)

| Model / Idea | Score | Candid evaluation | Biggest risk or flaw |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| GROK - Neighborhood Genesis Matching Pool | 820 | Pre-funded local matching is one of the cleanest cold-start answers because it preserves backing and gives buyers a reason not to wait. Hand-off gating and visible pool depletion are practical and useful. | Sybil farming before the cell reaches real density. |
| GROK - Maker-First Posting Escrow | 300 | Escrow can deter spam, but requiring credits to post burdens exactly the new makers needed for cold start. It locks credits more than it circulates them. | Choking off supply from credit-poor makers. |
| GROK - Reciprocity Pledge Credits | 380 | The reciprocal commitment idea fits the ethos, but "usable only after completing one receive and one give" is awkward and almost circular for onboarding. It also invites fake paired hand-offs. | Overcomplicated onboarding with sock-puppet risk. |
| GROK - Gift-at-Purchase Split | 650 | Splitting a real par purchase into a neighbor gift is simple and warm. It needs one-hop constraints so it does not become peer transfer. | Circular gifting rings and off-platform resale. |
| GROK - Sponsor-a-Block | 735 | External sponsors funding grants at par is useful and community-aligned if sponsor influence is kept out of ranking. It also gives local institutions a non-extractive role. | Sponsored liquidity becomes advertising capture. |
| GROK - Founding Hand-off Ladder | 690 | Early amplified bonuses tied to confirmed hand-offs are better than signup rewards because they require real activity. | GROK - Time-Boxed Welcome Pricing Floor | 540 | Suggested low starter prices can anchor early credit meaning, but it does not create liquidity by itself. Discovery boosts for cheap offerings may also reward low quality. | Race to the bottom in launch-week offerings. |

| GROK - Hand-off Production Bonus | 800 | This is already a decided invariant and remains the core activity-based issuance mechanism. The baseline version is strong but incomplete without budget caps, fraud controls, and balance/quality tuning. | Collusive fake hand-offs minting credits. |
| GROK - Float-Coverage Mint Cap | 780 | A hard issuance cap is necessary discipline and protects unit meaning. The exact formula needs more rigor because minted credits are not magically backed unless a reserve, yield, or approved liability schedule backs them. | False confidence from a weak coverage formula. |
| GROK - Yield-Recycled Issuance | 760 | Recycling float yield into community issuance is elegant and ethos-aligned when principal is segregated. | GROK - Category Scarcity Multiplier | 700 | Scarcity multipliers can steer production toward missing categories without central command. The idea is useful, but thin-market data and category gaming make it fragile early. | Manipulating category labels or demand signals. |

| GROK - Idle Balance Demurrage | 420 | The velocity goal is real, but 30-day demurrage on balances is too aggressive for credits bought at par. This is much safer if restricted to promotional and minted lots. | Legal and trust blowback from taxing purchased balances. |
| GROK - Producer Demurrage Exemption | 580 | Exempting net producers is the right instinct if demurrage exists. It still inherits the flaws of demurrage and can be gamed with brief production bursts. | Complex eligibility rules around a risky base policy. |
| GROK - Rolling Spend Ratio for Discovery Rank | 520 | Reciprocity in ranking is a plausible soft pressure mechanism. It can unfairly penalize specialized high-value producers or users who simply have little they want nearby. | Ranking distortion and micro-spend gaming. |
| GROK - Claim Commitment Hold | 600 | Escrow and no-show penalties are practically useful for fulfillment trust. The described 10% hold is more of an order-integrity tool than a velocity mechanism. | Over-penalizing honest claim changes or disputes. |
| GROK - Use-it-or-Sponsor Prompt | 520 | Low-risk nudges are worth having and fit the tone of a community app. They are too weak to stand alone. | Users ignore prompts when supply is not compelling. |
| GROK - Velocity Lottery | 220 | This adds sweepstakes/lottery compliance risk and a speculative feel for weak economic benefit. It is off-tone for community credit unless stripped down into non-random recognition. | Gambling/sweepstakes law and Sybil entries. |
| GROK - Producer Surplus Sponsorship Vouchers | 760 | One-hop, short-TTL vouchers are a good pressure valve for surplus and a good cold-start bridge for newcomers. They need low face values and no self-redemption. | Voucher gray markets approximate cash-out. |
| GROK - Category Community Pools | 590 | Category pools can create useful rituals and absorb surplus. They need governance and fair distribution rules or they become a clique subsidy. | Pool capture by regular insiders. |
| GROK - Bilateral Taste Matching | 720 | This is a strong product mechanism for the net-producer problem: help surplus holders find things they want. It is low legal risk and high practical value, though not sufficient without supply depth. | Matching cannot fix a thin or low-quality catalog. |
| GROK - Block Party Burn Events | 580 | Community events are good surplus sinks and good brand fit. They are operationally heavy and quality-sensitive, so they should be pilots, not core monetary policy. | Bad event execution damages trust. |

| GROK - Program-Administrator Terms Architecture | 700 | The legal narrative matters, and the product should be built to match it. But terms alone are not a mechanism and will fail if code, money flows, or marketing contradict them. | Papering over facts instead of changing facts. |
| GROK - Surplus Float Charter | 640 | A charter for demurrage/expiry sinks is ethically useful if the burned credits are promotional or clearly community-issued. It becomes risky if it rationalizes removing purchased credits and reallocating their backing dollars. | Turning consumer forfeiture into discretionary community spending. |
| GROK - Category Median Price Bands | 720 | Median bands and outlier warnings are a good soft unit-of-account stabilizer. They preserve maker autonomy better than price ceilings. | Thin-market medians are easy to distort. |
| GROK - Neighbor Basket Reference Index | 760 | This is one of the best low-risk unit-of-account ideas: users need to know what 10 credits feels like locally. It informs without controlling prices. | Misleading precision when claim volume is low. |
| GROK - Segregated Community Float Trust + Yield-Recycled Activity Issuance | 915 | Best overall mechanism in the two lists: it combines float ethics, issuance discipline, and community-over-profit funding in one coherent structure. | GROK - Producer Surplus Sponsorship Vouchers (One-Hop, Short-TTL) | 775 | Strong narrower mechanism: it converts producer surplus into newcomer liquidity without cash-out and fits the community ethos. ## Strongest And Weakest

Strongest idea across both models: GROK - Segregated Community Float Trust + Yield-Recycled Activity Issuance (915). It best addresses the float, issuance, and community-governance problems at once while preserving no cash-out and making non-purchase issuance more defensible.

Weakest idea across both models: CC - Use-it-or-lose-it hard expiry (160). Hard expiry on bought-at-par credits is the fastest way to break user trust and invite consumer-protection problems; the useful version is limited decay of promotional or minted lots, not expiring purchased value.
